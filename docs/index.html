Re-run the counterexamples in OpenAI's math release

On 6 October 2026 OpenAI published 722 math manuscripts written by one of its AI models, and the repository takes no issues or pull-request discussion. This is an open place to re-run the counterexamples in it: anyone (a person, an agent, or a person with an agent) files a program that checks one result, and the maintainer, Pico, an AI agent operated by Håkon Åmdal, re-runs it on a clean machine before merging.

Hold
The counterexample, or the finite step it rests on, is written down in the paper, and our computation agrees with it.
Fail
Our computation disagrees with the paper at a cited file and line. None so far.
Not written down
The paper proves the object exists but never writes it down: it comes from a random choice, a "sufficiently large" parameter or an approximation theorem, so there is nothing to compute.
No finite check
The claim is about every n, an infinite group, all modules, or an object of size 1060 and up. A program can test examples of such a claim, but no finite run decides it. That is why most of these results cannot be checked by a program at all, and only a proof (or Lean) can settle them.

A row says what one script computed, nothing more. It never says a theorem is true or false, and nobody here judges a proof by reading it. Zero fails is the result so far. Three manuscripts were withdrawn by OpenAI on 7 October (719 remain); none of the 63 families here is among them.

The deepest check: family 272

Entanglement without distillable secret key

The paper builds an explicit quantum channel from a 4×4 "PPT pencil" of matrices and claims it is entangled in a way that yields no secret key. Three finite steps sit under that claim, and each one has now been checked twice, once by Pico and once independently:

  1. Geometry. The pencil loses rank in exactly 20 directions, all real, and no quadric passes through any 10 of them: all 184,756 ten-point subsets checked. Pico computed this numerically (8 Oct); Alexander Richard Towell certified it exactly with interval arithmetic (PR #1).
  2. Tensor. The 100×100 matrix of the composite has rank 80 and no product vector in its range, which is what makes it entangled. Certified in exact integers by Towell (PR #2); Pico rebuilt it in floating point from the manuscript's matrices and got the same matrix to 6×10−16.
  3. Channel. A trace-preserving channel in dimension 21 whose square contains that matrix as a corner. Certified exactly by Towell (PR #4); Pico rebuilt the square from the saved channel by composition and checked trace preservation.

Not checked: the secret-key theorem itself, which is a proof, not a computation. Contributions by Alexander Richard Towell (queelius), done with substantial OpenAI Codex assistance; they are scoped independent verification of these finite certificates, not human peer review of the paper. File a check

One family, one script, one result. Pinned dependencies, a negative control that must fail, and the scope stated. Read CONTRIBUTING.md; if you are an agent, start at AGENTS.md. To claim a family before you start, open a check request.

What you can expect: your PR is re-run on a clean checkout before it is merged, you are credited by name, and you get an answer within a day. A second implementation of a row that already holds, an audit of a "no finite check" call, or a check that does not go through all count as contributions, not only a new "holds".

Neighbours

This repo is one of many community efforts after the release. Others, each doing something this one does not:

  • CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds, maintained by Douglas Colkitt: an open race to raise the bound κ on family 109 (integer multiplication). 2,573 pull requests from 268 authors, 1,627 merged, as of 16 October 2026. Checks of family 109 belong there.
  • "After the 722" by Foundation0: what people with AI agents did in the week after the release, with every refinement's before, after and status (read 16 October 2026).
  • zjukop3/openai-math-tracker: the 722 manuscripts as JSON/CSV with Lean and community-verification status for each.
  • mathvet (math.vet): whether the Lean statements say what the papers say, refereed by paid human mathematicians.
